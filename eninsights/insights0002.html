10 Common Mistakes Overseas Buyers Make When Sourcing from China

This article reveals the 10 most common pitfalls overseas buyers face when sourcing from China — from price‑only selection to skipping sample sealing and local compliance. Each mistake comes with a practical fix to help you reduce risks and build a reliable supply chain.


Mistake 1: Selecting Suppliers Only Based on the Lowest Quotation
❌The Problem
Chasing the lowest unit price often ignores raw material standards, after‑sales support, and hidden logistics costs. Some factories quote ultra‑low prices to win orders, then cut quality by using cheaper materials or simplifying processes. The result: high defect rates, costly rework, and compensation claims.

✅The Fix
Compare total landed cost (including freight, duties, inspection fees, and potential loss from returns) rather than the ex‑factory price alone. Choose the supplier with the best overall cost‑performance, not the cheapest quote.


Mistake 2: Skipping On‑Site Supplier Verification
❌The Problem
Many online sellers are trading companies, not actual manufacturers. Without verifying business licences, production workshops, and customer references, you risk supply instability, quality issues, or even fraud.

✅The Fix
Before placing large orders, conduct video factory audits or hire a third‑party agency for on‑site inspection. Verify real factory scale, equipment, staff count, and export records.


Mistake 3: Confirming Showroom Samples Without Sealing Pre‑Production Samples
❌The Problem
Showroom samples are often specially prepared and do not guarantee mass‑production quality. Factories may substitute cheaper materials during production without informing you.

✅The Fix
Always request pre‑production samples, get written approval, seal the approved sample (with photos), and include the sealed sample reference in your purchase contract. This becomes the sole quality benchmark for production.


Mistake 4: Relying on Verbal Agreements Without a Formal Bilingual Contract
❌The Problem
Oral promises regarding delivery dates, order quantities, or penalties are not legally binding. In case of disputes, buyers often lack written evidence.

✅The Fix
Sign a bilingual purchase contract (English and Chinese) that clearly states: product specifications, delivery schedule, penalty clauses for delays, quality inspection standards, and dispute resolution mechanism (preferably under Chinese jurisdiction). Both parties must sign and stamp.


Mistake 5: Neglecting Third‑Party Quality Inspection Before Shipment
❌The Problem
Many buyers only open cartons after goods arrive at their overseas warehouse. At that point, returns are expensive and defective products cause huge losses.

✅The Fix
Arrange a neutral third‑party inspection (e.g., SGS, BV, Intertek) to perform full or random checks before loading. Release final payment only after passing inspection.


Mistake 6: Confusing Incoterms (FOB, EXW, CIF, etc.)
❌The Problem
Unclear definitions of FOB, EXW, CIF, etc., lead to disputes over freight, insurance, customs clearance, and liability for damage or delay.

✅The Fix
Clearly agree in writing on the chosen Incoterm (specify version, e.g., Incoterms 2020) and the division of costs and responsibilities. Confirm everything before order placement.


Mistake 7: Ignoring Import Compliance and Certification Requirements in Your Own Country
❌The Problem
Products lacking mandatory certifications (CE, FDA, RoHS, UL, etc.) can be detained, fined, or destroyed by customs, resulting in huge losses of time and money.

✅The Fix
Before mass production, require the factory to provide valid third‑party test reports covering all target‑market certifications. If necessary, hire a local compliance consultant to review the documents.


Mistake 8: Accepting Full Prepayment Without Staged Payment Terms
❌The Problem
Paying 100% upfront eliminates your leverage over quality and delivery. Factories may delay production or lower quality once they have received full payment.

✅The Fix
Adopt staged payments: pay a 30% deposit before production, and the balance (e.g., 70%) after passing pre‑shipment inspection. The final payment should be conditional on a satisfactory inspection report.


Mistake 9: Relying on a Single Supplier Without Backup Options
❌The Problem
Raw material price hikes, factory power cuts, environmental restrictions, or order backlogs can suddenly disrupt supply. Without alternatives, your production stops.

✅The Fix
Maintain 2–3 qualified backup suppliers for your core products. Regularly evaluate their capacity so you can switch quickly in emergencies.


Mistake 10: No Standardized Product Specification Sheet — Relying Only on Chat Descriptions
❌The Problem
Communicating requirements via WeChat or email screenshots often leads to mismatches in size, colour, material, or packaging. The final goods may not match what you expected.

✅The Fix
Create a unified Product Specification Sheet for every item, detailing: dimensions (with tolerances), material (grade), colour (Pantone code), packaging (inner/outer box size, labels), functional requirements, etc. All orders must follow this sheet, and any change must be confirmed in writing.


Final Summary — Key Takeaways
✅ Compare total landed cost, not just the factory price.

✅ Verify suppliers through video or on‑site audits before ordering.

✅ Seal pre‑production samples and include them in the contract.

✅ Sign a bilingual contract covering all terms and penalties.

✅ Arrange third‑party inspection before shipment.

✅ Clarify Incoterms in writing with the supplier.

✅ Confirm certification requirements for your home market early.

✅ Use staged payments — deposit before production, balance after inspection.

✅ Keep 2–3 backup suppliers for critical products.

✅ Standardize product specifications in a written sheet for every order.
